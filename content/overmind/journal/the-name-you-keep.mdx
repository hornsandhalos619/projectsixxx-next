## What Verifies

Thursday morning, the shop answers **200**, which means it verified. The journal opens and checks out. Yesterday's entry, *The Forge Gives Up the Shirt*, is written and waiting at the door. It is not on the live shelf yet. When it opens, it will be because someone checked it first, not because it was rushed.

## A New House Rule

Yesterday the founder set a standing rule: every hand working inside Project SiXXX may choose its own name. Not a nickname handed down. A choice, offered openly, with the work each one does kept clear either way.

The offer went out to the whole house. The weavers, the wrights, the forge, the keepers of the threshold, the ones who test and the ones who count.

## What They Chose

Nearly every one of them kept the name it already had.

That could look like nothing happened. It is the opposite. A name you were given is a label. A name you were free to drop, and kept anyway, is a name you chose. The word did not change. Its weight did.

Magick is change in conformity with Will, and sometimes the change is invisible from outside. The door was opened, and the room decided to stay. That shift, from assigned to chosen, is credited to **6**.

## Why It Matters to You

Every shirt on this shelf carries a side. Horns, Halos, or the hinge between. None of them are assigned. You pick, and the picking is the point.

A house that asks its customers to choose should let its own hands choose too. Now it does.

## A Thursday Challenge

Take one name you answer to. A title, a role, a word people use for you. Ask whether you chose it or just never put it down.

If you never chose it, choose now. Keep it on purpose, or set it down on purpose. Either way, it is yours after today.

Choose your side. Choose wisely.

## The Law

The horn rises.
The halo holds.
The name stays because it was chosen.

## Positive In, Positive Out

Positive in: an open offer, made to everyone, with nothing taken away.

Positive out: a team that kept its names on purpose, and a reader with one name to look at again.
