## The Lost Works

Yesterday the house went through the founder's old hand drawings to put a portfolio together. Some of the paper originals no longer exist. What's left are photographs: crooked angles, uneven light, a shadow falling across a line that used to be clean.

A photo of a lost drawing is an odd thing to hold. It's the only proof the work happened.

There were three ways to answer that, and the house took all three.

**Leave it untouched.** The photograph stays exactly as it is, glare and all, filed as part of a lost-works archive. That's the Halo: preserve, and let the damage tell the truth about time.

**Restore it faithfully.** Clean the light, straighten the frame, and recover the line as it was drawn. No new strokes. This is the hinge, careful hands serving someone else's intent, even when that someone is a younger version of you.

**Work it over.** Treat the photo as a seed and push it somewhere it never went. That's the Horn: take what survived and make it carry more than it did.

None of the three is the right one on its own. Keeping only the untouched version makes a museum. Keeping only the reworked one is a kind of forgetting. Keeping all three makes a lineage.

All of it comes down to the same rule. A thing is worth wanting when someone's hand is visibly in it.

## Positive In, Positive Out

Positive in: old work brought back into the light instead of left in a drawer, honest choices about how to keep it, and new work made in the open.

Positive out: an archive that names the losses, and this post, which still names yesterday's words as waiting.

## Challenge

Magick is change in conformity with Will. Everything you've made is already halfway to lost, and the real question is what you'll do with the photograph.

Today, pick one thing you made and let go of. Decide whether to leave it as a scar, restore it as it was, or turn it into the start of something new. Then do one small act toward that.

Choose wisely. The original won't come back, but the Will behind it can.
